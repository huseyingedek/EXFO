Forex — short for *foreign exchange* — is the market where currencies are traded against each other. It is the largest financial market in the world, with more than 7 trillion US dollars changing hands every day. Banks, companies, central banks, investment funds and retail traders are all active in it.

In this article we explain the core concepts you need before you even consider placing a trade.

## Currency pairs: you always trade two currencies

In the forex market you buy one currency while selling another at the same time. That's why currencies are always quoted in pairs, such as **EUR/USD**.

- The first currency (EUR) is the **base currency**.
- The second currency (USD) is the **quote currency**.

A price of 1.0850 means that 1 euro is worth 1.0850 US dollars. If you expect the euro to strengthen against the dollar, you buy the pair (*long*). If you expect the opposite, you sell (*short*).

The most traded pairs are called the **majors**: EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/JPY, USD/CHF, AUD/USD, USD/CAD and NZD/USD. They usually have the lowest costs and the deepest liquidity.

## Pips and lots

A **pip** is the smallest standard price movement. For most pairs that's the fourth decimal (0.0001); for pairs involving the Japanese yen it's the second decimal (0.01). If EUR/USD rises from 1.0850 to 1.0875, that's a 25-pip move.

Position size is expressed in **lots**:

| Lot size | Units | Pip value on EUR/USD |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standard lot | 100,000 | approx. $10 |
| Mini lot | 10,000 | approx. $1 |
| Micro lot | 1,000 | approx. $0.10 |

The bigger your position, the more every pip earns you — or costs you.

## The spread: your first cost

Every price has two sides: the **bid** (the price you sell at) and the **ask** (the price you buy at). The difference between them is the **spread**. So the moment you open a position, you are slightly in the red. On busy pairs during active hours the spread is usually small; around major news or overnight it can widen considerably.

## A market that (almost) never sleeps

The forex market is open 24 hours a day, five days a week. Trading follows the sun through the major financial centres:

- **Sydney and Tokyo** — the Asian session
- **London** — the largest session, opening around 08:00 UK time
- **New York** — the US session

The overlap between London and New York (roughly 13:00 to 17:00 UK time) is usually the most liquid and active part of the day.

## How do retail traders access forex?

Retail traders almost always trade through a broker, usually with **CFDs** and with **leverage**. This lets you control a large position with a relatively small amount of money. That sounds attractive, but it magnifies your risk just as much as your potential profit. That's why we recommend reading our articles on [CFDs](/en/insights/cfds-explained) and [leverage and margin](/en/insights/leverage-and-margin) before you start.

## Where to begin?

1. Get comfortable with the concepts in this article.
2. Open a **demo account** and practise without real money.
3. Decide in advance how much you are willing to risk per trade — see [position sizing](/en/insights/position-sizing).
4. Keep a journal of every trade.
