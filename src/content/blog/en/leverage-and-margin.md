**Leverage** lets you open a position that is much larger than the amount you put up yourself. That is exactly what makes forex and CFD trading attractive — and what makes it so risky. In this article we explain how leverage and margin fit together.

## What is margin?

**Margin** is the amount your broker holds as collateral to keep a position open. It is not a cost, but a kind of deposit.

With leverage of **1:30** you only need to hold 1/30 of the position value as margin, or roughly **3.33%**.

**Example:** you want to open a €10,000 position in EUR/USD.

- With 1:30 leverage, the required margin is €10,000 ÷ 30 ≈ **€333**.
- So with €333 you control a €10,000 position.

## Leverage cuts both ways

If the price moves 1% in your favour, the value of your position rises by €100 — that's **30% of your margin**. If the price moves 1% against you, you lose that same €100. A small price move therefore has a big effect on your account.

## European leverage limits

In the EU, retail investors are subject to maximum leverage per type of underlying asset:

| Underlying asset | Maximum leverage |
| --- | --- |
| Major currency pairs | 1:30 |
| Other currency pairs, gold and major indices | 1:20 |
| Other commodities and minor indices | 1:10 |
| Individual shares | 1:5 |
| Cryptocurrencies | 1:2 |

These are maximums. Being *allowed* to use 1:30 leverage doesn't mean it's wise to do so.

## Margin call and stop-out

Your **margin level** shows how much room you have left: the equity in your account divided by the margin in use.

- When your positions are losing, your margin level falls.
- Many brokers warn you at a certain level with a **margin call**.
- Under European rules, the broker must close positions when your equity falls to **50%** of the required margin. This is called a **stop-out** or *margin close-out*.

On top of that, **negative balance protection** applies: as a retail investor you cannot lose more than the money in your account.

## Effective leverage: what professionals do

More important than the maximum leverage is your **effective leverage**: the total value of your open positions divided by your account balance. If you have €5,000 in your account and open positions worth €25,000, your effective leverage is 1:5 — even if your broker allows 1:30.

Experienced traders deliberately keep their effective leverage low and size positions based on **risk per trade**, not on available margin. Learn how in [position sizing](/en/insights/position-sizing).

## In short

- Margin is collateral, not a cost.
- Leverage magnifies profits and losses equally.
- Keep your effective leverage low and always use a stop-loss.
- Practise on a demo account first until you truly understand the mechanics.
