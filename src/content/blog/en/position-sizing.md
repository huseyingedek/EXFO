Most new traders think first about *where* to enter. Professional traders think first about *how much* they are risking. Position sizing may well be the most important skill in trading — and one of the easiest to learn.

## Why risk per trade matters so much

Every trader faces losing streaks. The question isn't whether they will come, but whether your account survives them. Look at what ten losses in a row do at different risk levels:

| Risk per trade | Account after 10 losses in a row |
| --- | --- |
| 1% | approx. 90% (−9.6%) |
| 2% | approx. 82% (−18.3%) |
| 5% | approx. 60% (−40.1%) |
| 10% | approx. 35% (−65.1%) |

And recovering is harder than it looks: after a 50% loss you need a **100% gain** just to get back to where you started.

## The 1% rule

Many traders follow the rule of thumb of risking **no more than 1% (2% at most)** of their account per trade. ‘Risking’ here means the amount you lose if your stop-loss is hit.

## The formula

To calculate your position size you need three things:

1. Your **risk amount** (for example 1% of your account)
2. The distance to your **stop-loss** in pips
3. The **pip value** per lot

> **Position size (in lots) = risk amount ÷ (stop-loss in pips × pip value per lot)**

## Worked example

- Account balance: **€5,000**
- Risk per trade: 1% = **€50**
- Trade: EUR/USD, stop-loss at **25 pips**
- Pip value per standard lot: $10, at a rate of 1.08 roughly **€9.26**

Position size = €50 ÷ (25 × €9.26) ≈ **0.21 lots**

Always round down. If your stop-loss is hit, you lose about €50 — exactly the risk you chose in advance, plus any costs.

Note: with a wider stop-loss your position automatically becomes smaller. That way your risk stays the same, whatever the market conditions.

## Risk versus reward

Combine position sizing with a fixed **risk/reward ratio**. Suppose you win on average twice as much as you lose (1:2) and win 40% of your trades:

- 4 winning trades × 2R = **+8R**
- 6 losing trades × 1R = **−6R**
- Result over 10 trades: **+2R**

So even with fewer than half of your trades winning, a strategy can be positive — provided your risk per trade is consistent. Unfortunately the reverse is also true: without fixed rules, a few large losses can wipe out everything. And past results are no guarantee of future performance.

## Checklist for every trade

- Do I know where my stop-loss is before I enter?
- Have I calculated my position size instead of guessing it?
- Am I risking no more than 1–2% of my account?
- Have I included the costs (spread, swap)?
- Is there major news on the [economic calendar](/en/insights/economic-calendar)?
