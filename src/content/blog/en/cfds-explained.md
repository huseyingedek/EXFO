A **CFD** (*Contract for Difference*) is a contract between you and a broker that lets you speculate on the price movement of an underlying asset — such as a currency pair, an index, gold or oil — without owning that asset. You make or lose the **difference** between the price when you open and when you close your position.

## Long and short

With a CFD you can trade both rising and falling prices:

- If you expect a rise, you open a **buy** position (*long*).
- If you expect a fall, you open a **sell** position (*short*).

That's flexible, but it also means you can lose money in either direction if the market moves against you.

## A worked example

Suppose an index CFD is at 20,000 points and you buy a position of €1 per point.

- The index rises to 20,100 → your result is **+100 points × €1 = +€100**.
- The index falls to 19,900 → your result is **−100 points × €1 = −€100**.

Costs are not included yet.

## What costs do you pay?

1. **Spread** — the difference between the bid and the ask price. You pay it on every trade.
2. **Commission** — some brokers charge a fixed amount per trade or per lot.
3. **Overnight financing (swap)** — if you hold a position overnight, you pay or receive a fee. For positions held for weeks, this can add up quickly.
4. **Currency conversion** — if you trade a product that is not priced in your account currency, conversion costs may apply.

Costs look small per trade, but over time they make up a large part of your results. Always include them in your trading plan.

## European rules for retail investors

Since 2018, stricter rules have applied in the EU to CFDs offered to retail investors. They were introduced by ESMA and are enforced in the Netherlands by the AFM. The most important ones:

- **Maximum leverage** — for example 1:30 on major currency pairs and 1:20 on gold and major indices.
- **Margin close-out** — the broker must close positions when your margin falls to 50% of the required margin.
- **Negative balance protection** — you cannot lose more than the money in your account.
- **Standardised risk warning** — brokers must show the percentage of their clients who lose money.
- **No bonuses** — brokers may not lure you in with bonuses or gifts.

## The risks at a glance

- **Leverage** magnifies both profits and losses. A small price move can cost a large part of your deposit.
- **Gaps** — around news or over the weekend the price can ‘jump’, so your stop-loss may be filled at a worse price.
- **Counterparty risk** — your contract runs through your broker. Always choose a licensed provider.
- **Complexity** — precisely because CFDs are so easy to access, their risks are often underestimated.

## What to look for in a broker

- A licence from the **AFM** or another European regulator. You can check this in the public register on the regulator's website.
- Transparent costs (spreads, commissions, swaps).
- Negative balance protection.
- Be wary of firms that approach you via social media or promise high returns; regulators publish warnings about unlicensed firms.
