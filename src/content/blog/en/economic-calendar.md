Exchange rates react to economic news. A central bank rate decision or a surprising inflation figure can move a currency pair by dozens of pips within seconds. The **economic calendar** helps you see those moments coming.

## What is the economic calendar?

An overview of scheduled releases and events, such as interest rate decisions, employment figures and inflation data. For each release you'll usually see:

- the **time** of publication;
- the **expected impact** (low, medium or high);
- the **previous value**, the **forecast** (consensus) and — once released — the **actual value**.

## High-impact events

- **Interest rate decisions** by the European Central Bank (ECB), the US Federal Reserve (Fed) and other central banks, including the press conference that follows.
- **Non-Farm Payrolls (NFP)** — the US jobs report, usually released on the first Friday of the month at 08:30 New York time.
- **Inflation data (CPI)** — crucial for expectations about future interest rates.
- **GDP figures** and **purchasing managers' indices (PMI)** — gauges of economic growth.

## Why does the price move?

The market reacts not so much to the number itself, but to the difference from the **forecast**. A strong figure that was already fully priced in can even lead to a decline. That's why it's important to look not only at the result, but also at the consensus and at how the market reacts.

## The risks around news

- **Wider spreads** — just before and after major news, trading costs often rise sharply.
- **Slippage** — your order is filled at a worse price than expected, including your stop-loss.
- **Erratic moves** — price sometimes shoots one way first, then the other.

## How to use the calendar

1. **Check the calendar every morning** as a fixed part of your routine. Note the key releases for the currencies you follow.
2. **Define in your trading plan** what you do around news: for example, no new positions from 15 minutes before a major release.
3. **Reduce your risk** if you do hold a position, or close it beforehand.
4. **Let the dust settle.** After the first reaction, a clearer picture often emerges.
5. **Mind the time zone.** In spring and autumn, when daylight saving time in Europe and the US is out of sync, US releases shift by an hour.

Fundamental and technical analysis complement each other: the calendar tells you *when* the market may move, your analysis helps you understand *how* prepared you are for it.
